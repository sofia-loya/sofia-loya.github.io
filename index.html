BrainSight — Medtronic Intern Hackathon

🏆 1st Place · Neuromodulation

As part of the Medtronic Intern Hackathon, my team (YottaBrainz) built BrainSight, a closed-loop adaptive Deep Brain Stimulation (aDBS) parameter simulator for Parkinson's disease — and won first place in the neuromodulation track.

Traditional DBS runs at fixed settings around the clock, like a porch light left on all day. BrainSight demonstrates the smarter, closed-loop alternative: it reads a brain biomarker (beta-band activity in the subthalamic nucleus), and turns stimulation up when symptoms rise and down when they settle — like a thermostat. The simulator runs three virtual patients side by side (no DBS, fixed DBS, and adaptive DBS) so you can watch the adaptive approach hold symptoms in check while using noticeably less energy — the "same relief, less battery" story that matters for an implanted device. An optional Bluetooth link even drives a physical Arduino stimulator demo whose LED tracks the live stimulation level in real time.

The first-place prize was a photo with Medtronic Chairman & CEO Geoff Martha.
