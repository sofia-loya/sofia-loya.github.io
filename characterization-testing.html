Medical Device Reliability Engineering Test Method Development

Preparation & Execution of Characterization Testing

As a Reliability Engineering intern, I drove the development of six test methods for a pre-commercial cardiac ablation catheter — building the risk-based test strategy that prepares the device for Design Verification and EU clinical-trial readiness.

// situation

A smaller catheter that couldn't reuse its predecessor's tests

A pre-commercial, small-form-factor cardiac ablation catheter needed characterization testing to prepare for Design Verification (DV) ahead of EU clinical trials. Because the device was a smaller redesign of an existing platform, its inherited test methods couldn't simply be reused — they had to be reassessed and adapted to the new design before any verification results could be trusted.

// task

Own the test methods that move the project toward verification

Drive the development of six test methods for characterization testing: identify which specifications mattered most for patient safety, adapt each method to the new design, and help sequence and execute the testing so results would represent real-world performance.

// action

A four-step, risk-first approach

  • Risk analysis. Evaluated the Design FMEA (DFMEA) and Hazard Analysis with the team to isolate the safety-critical risks — those above a defined Risk-Before-Mitigation threshold and severity rating — then traced each risk back to the specifications that needed to be evaluated.
  • Assess & modify test methods. Adapted methods from a larger-form-factor predecessor to the new catheter, updating them for design differences such as the reduced electrode count, and rewrote them so an operator with no prior knowledge could follow along — adding diagrams, photos, and clearer instructions, then validating through dry runs. For example, I revised dielectric strength testing with an updated test voltage and a newly designed fixture for the smaller electrode groupings.
  • Test flow development. Collaborated with another intern to sequence the full test flow around two hard constraints — the destructive nature of certain tests and limited catheter availability — so the order reflected real clinical use and produced results representative of field performance.
  • Execution. Gathered materials, organized the lab space, and executed characterization testing alongside the team.
// result

A test strategy the team carries into Design Verification

  • The six test methods and their fixtures were adopted as the basis for Design Verification going forward.
  • The risk-based selection and test flow became the process the team continues to use into DV.
  • Assessed test-method reproducibility to ensure consistent, reliable execution before verification.
  • The work directly supports readiness for EU clinical trials, and ultimately human use.
